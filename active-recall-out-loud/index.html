The practical difference

Recognition feels easier than recall.

When you reread a line, the information may look familiar. That does not always mean you could produce it without the cue. Active recall removes the answer first and asks you to retrieve it. Speaking the answer adds a concrete output: you either produced the key idea or you did not.

Rereading

You see the question and answer together. Familiarity can be mistaken for mastery.

Silent recall

You hide the answer and think it through. This works, but it is easy to accept an incomplete thought.

Spoken recall

You say a complete response before the answer appears. The app can capture what the browser heard and compare it with your saved answer.

Designed around imperfect speech recognition

The browser can be wrong. You stay in control.

Technical vocabulary, accents, abbreviations, and background noise can confuse speech recognition. same3le treats automatic matching as a study convenience rather than an unquestionable grade.

Multiple alternatives

The app can consider several transcript alternatives returned by the browser instead of relying only on the first guess.

Flexible matching

Add accepted paraphrases and choose flexible, normal, or exact matching based on the material.

Manual correction

Select “I was correct” when the browser misheard you, retry the question, or continue after reviewing the full answer.

When active recall out loud is useful

  • Definitions and key terms
  • Mechanisms, pathways, and ordered steps
  • Language vocabulary and translations
  • Oral examination practice
  • Interview questions
  • Professional certification review

When another mode may be better

  • When you are in a quiet shared space
  • When the answer is a drawing, calculation, or long essay
  • When the browser does not support speech recognition
  • When you only want to hear the material during a walk
  • When the screen must be locked—use experimental continuous review instead

Use it well

How to make spoken recall less frustrating

Use short prompts

Ask one clear thing at a time. Long multi-part questions are harder to answer and harder to match reliably.

Add synonyms

Include common abbreviations, alternate spellings, and equivalent phrases in the accepted-alternatives column.

Use the right strictness

Flexible matching fits concept review. Exact matching is better when the precise wording itself matters.

Frequently asked

Active recall FAQ

Is verbal active recall better than silent recall?

They are both retrieval practice. Speaking can make an incomplete answer more obvious, but it is not necessary for every topic or every student. same3le provides spoken and non-microphone modes so you can choose.

Does same3le understand the meaning of every answer?

No. The current matcher uses local text-based heuristics and accepted alternatives. It is not a validated semantic grading system and should not be treated as one.

Can the app keep the phone awake?

In supported browsers, Answer out loud can request Screen Wake Lock while the session is running. Device and battery settings may still prevent or interrupt it.

Is the active-recall app free?

Yes. The current core experience is free and does not require payment information. Optional Pro account features are planned separately.
